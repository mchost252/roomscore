Yes. Now that we’ve settled the 9 parent trophy categories, I’d structure the entire Krios achievement archive like this.

The important part: the parent is the visual family/style, while the trophies underneath are progressively stronger versions of that same idea. That gives you a clean system where every category can have its own recognizable visual language.

Also, I would not attach XP to every trophy. The trophy itself is the recognition; XP/rank can remain a separate system if you decide to use one. That avoids turning Krios into a points-chasing game, which is especially important for a productivity app. 

🏆 KRIOS TROPHY ARCHIVE

01 — BEGINNING

Theme: Starting, taking the first step, entering the Krios journey.

This should be the easiest category. It rewards starting, not mastery.

Trophy	Requirement

First Win	Complete your first meaningful task
First Session	Complete your first Focus session
First Rhythm	Be active on Krios for 3 separate days

Krios Initiate	Reach 100 completed tasks


Design progression: tiny → small → established.

One important design decision

I wouldn't make every category have exactly the same number of trophies.

That would make the system feel artificially generated.

For example:

Beginning: ~8

Consistency: ~12–14

Task Master: ~12

Focused: ~10–12

Community: ~8–10

Communication: ~8

Growth: ~8

Learning: ~8–10

Legendary: ~8


That gives you roughly 80–90 meaningful trophies, rather than 100+ trophies that users barely care about.

And your existing parent icons become the visual DNA for each entire family. So when you later design "First Step," "First Win," "Getting Started," etc., they should all immediately look like they belong to the Beginning parent—even though each one is visually upgraded as the achievement becomes harder. That's the system I'd lock in before generating the individual trophy artwork.
